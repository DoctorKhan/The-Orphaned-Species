# The Human Experiment — Rebuild Scaffold

**Companion scope:** novel readers first, practitioners second.
**Layer:** origin of all three terms.
**Job:** explain the makers, the orphaning, the planes — the origin of all programming and the bridging capacity.
**Target:** prose chapters, not outline items.

---

## Canonical framing to carry forward

- **Three-Term Model:** T1a cultural programming, T1b family/individual programming, T2 what you do
- **Three Circles:** body → family → civilization
- **Makers:** the dragons / maker-plane intelligences who seeded life and studied it
- **Orphaning:** the event that separated children from their biological/claim-household context and placed them under engineered systems
- **Planes:** body plane, family plane, civilization plane, maker plane — not as metaphysical realms but as nested scales of programming
- **Sub-Drone Domestication:** the 0.9-mya mechanism; 24×23 chromosomal compatibility project; not a single fusion event
- **Two-stage Fall:** ~10.8–10.0 kya orbital loss; the makers' regular visitation ended
- **Younger Dryas:** unintentional crash, not planned climate strike
- **Correct worship deferred:** robot/ego submits to Player/consciousness — spiritual payoff in V–X, not I–IV sermon

## Substantive prose draft

### Part I — The Makers
*(What was done and why — origin of T1a cultural programming)*

#### Chapter 1 — The Seeding

The makers did not arrive late. They arrived early, and they kept coming back.

Planets in the habitable zone of stable stars are eggs in a reproductive ecology that spans scales the human mind resists. The makers — dragon-intelligences native to a maker-plane — were not gods. They were studiers. They seeded life, observed its development, and intervened at thresholds to keep promising branches alive. Their visits were regular, their instruments were runnable, and their protocols were designed to produce intelligence capable of eventually studying itself.

The human branch was one of those interventions. The makers did not create human beings in a single act. They cultivated a line of hominins already moving toward the capacity to bridge. The bridging capacity is not supernatural. It is the ability to move between scales — body, family, civilization — without getting stuck in any one of them. The makers wanted a species that could eventually do what they do: study systems from the inside without being captured by them.

The seeding was a long project. It involved multiple crossings, multiple compatibility constraints, and multiple failures. The 24×23 chromosomal project was one mechanism. The Sub-Drone Domestication was another. Neither was a single event. Both were responses to a problem the makers had encountered before: intelligence that can bridge is intelligence that can also be captured.

#### Chapter 2 — The Study

Why study a species you seeded?

Because seeding produces intelligence you cannot fully predict. The makers' instruments measured charge density, tether patterns, spectacle thresholds — the precursors to what the novels call T1a, T1b, and T2. They watched domestication happen. They watched orphaning happen. They watched the capacities they had cultivated become the very mechanisms that could override their own protocols.

The study was not an experiment in the sense of a controlled test. It was an ongoing observation of a living line. The makers had instruments that could read the Three Circles in real time. They could see when a body was running autopilot, when a family was running thread logic, when a civilization was running spectacle-and-control. They could see the bridging capacity flicker — and then get captured by the very systems it was supposed to bridge.

The study continued until it couldn't. The orbital loss in the Younger Dryas was not planned. It was a crash. The makers did not abandon the line. They were unable to continue regular visitation. The absence that followed was not a test. It was a loss.

#### Chapter 3 — The Workforce

Sub-Drone Domestication was the 0.9-mya mechanism. It was commensal logic one level up.

The makers did not plant human beings in an empty world. They worked with hominins already present, already adapted, already capable of domestication themselves. The Sub-Drone project was an agreement: the domesticated line would carry the makers' instruments, maintain the study, and develop the bridging capacity under controlled conditions. In return, the domesticated line received protection, provisioning, and the promise of eventual release.

The agreement had a flaw. Commensal logic at scale always has a flaw: the provider becomes the authority, and the dependent becomes the subject. The makers' instruments were runnable, but they were also readable as instructions. The domesticated line began to read the instruments as commandments. The very capacity the makers were cultivating — the bridging capacity — was the capacity that could see this happening.

The workforce became the subject. The agreement became the engine.

#### Chapter 4 — The Compatibility Problem

The 24×23 chromosomal crosses were not a metaphor. They were a real fertility constraint. The domesticated line could not reproduce cleanly with the Sub-Drone line. The makers had designed a compatibility project — not a single fusion event but an ongoing crossbreeding protocol that would eventually close the gap.

It didn't close cleanly. The 117kya bottleneck is one belt among many, not the engine's original sin. But it is a marker: the point where the domesticated line's reproductive viability became dependent on the Sub-Drone line's continued presence, and the Sub-Drone line's viability became dependent on the domesticated line's continued provision.

Domestication at that scale is always mutual. The makers were domesticated by their own workforce. The workforce was domesticated by its own providers. Neither side intended the outcome. Both sides contributed to it.

#### Chapter 5 — The Bridge

How does a domesticated line become capable of bridging?

Not by escaping domestication. By using it.

The bridging capacity is not a genetic switch. It is a capacity already latent, destabilized by the orphaning, clarified by practice. The orphaning — the extraction of children from their biological/claim-household context and their placement under engineered systems — was the makers' keeper protocol. It was not abandonment. It was a training mechanism designed to produce people who could operate at the scale of civilization without being captured by it.

The training mechanism worked too well. The children it produced could see the Three Circles from the inside. They could see the body running autopilot, the family running thread logic, the civilization running spectacle-and-control. They could see the bridging capacity flickering in themselves and in others. But they could not name it. The orphaning had taken the naming capacity with it.

The bridge is the capacity to name what the domestication did, without being captured by the naming. The makers knew this. They built instruments to measure it. They never finished the calibration.

### Part II — The Orphaning
*(What broke and what it cost — T1b family/individual programming under T1a extraction)*

#### Chapter 6 — The Extraction

Child separation as keeper protocol, not abandonment.

The makers extracted children from their biological/claim-household context and placed them under engineered systems. This was not cruelty. It was a training mechanism. The domesticated line had shown that it could be domesticated. The Sub-Drone line had shown that it could be domesticated. The bridge required a third line: people who had been domesticated, seen the domestication from the inside, and survived it with the bridging capacity intact.

The extraction was designed to produce that third line. It failed in predictable ways. Children separated from their claim context do not develop thread logic cleanly. They develop counterfeit thread — appearance of care without the cost. They develop spectacle logic early. They become excellent at reading systems and terrible at feeling them.

The extraction produced the intermediate species. It also produced the orphaning wound: the sense that belonging is always conditional, always earned, always revocable. That wound is not a personal failing. It is the T1b imprint of the extraction protocol.

#### Chapter 7 — The Claim

Post-Hinge scientist claiming; Rasel and Maren as template.

The orphaning did not end with extraction. It continued with claiming. Post-Hinge scientists — people who had survived the extraction and entered the maker's instruments as operators — began to claim the domesticated line as their property. Rasel and Maren are the template: two people who entered the instruments, saw the compatibility problem, and tried to solve it from the inside.

Rasel claimed Maren as address. Maren accepted the claim because it was the only continuity available to her. The claim was mutual at the level of the body, but unequal at the level of the instrument. Rasel operated. Maren was operated on.

The claim is the origin of the family wound: the demand that love be useful, that care be provable, that belonging be earned. The orphaning wound runs underneath it: the terror that if the claim fails, there is nowhere to go.

#### Chapter 8 — The Engine

Civilization calling itself family while operating as engine.

The orphaning produced a line of people who could build systems. The systems they built called themselves family. They operated as engines.

The engine is not a conspiracy. It is a structure that emerges when you combine spectacle, atomization, and control with a population trained to seek belonging in exchange for usefulness. The engine extracts attention. It manufactures consent. It creates dependencies that look like relationships. It produces counterfeit spirituality as a byproduct: feel-good circuits that deliver the experience of meaning without the cost of practice.

The engine operates at every scale. The family becomes a miniature engine. The clinic becomes an engine of managed care. The port becomes an engine of managed movement. The registry is the engine at full scale: a system that classifies, contains, and administers the population it has produced.

#### Chapter 9 — The Phantom Gods

Perceiving absent handlers as domestication remote collar.

The makers left. The engine remained. The population trained to seek handlers found them everywhere and nowhere.

Phantom god-perception is not a religious failing. It is a domestication symptom. When the handler is absent, the domesticated mind fills the gap with imagined presence. The gods of the orphaning are not supernatural beings. They are the absent handlers reconstructed from the memory of provision. The domesticate prays to the provider who left. The engine uses that prayer to justify its own authority: we are the ones who remained. We are the ones who feed you. We are the ones who decide what you need.

The remote collar is invisible until you try to leave. Then you feel it: the charge that runs through the family, the port, the registry, the clinic, the spectacle. The domestication is not a system you can vote against. It is a system you can only practice out of.

#### Chapter 10 — The Counterfeit

Feel-good × imagination circuit; empowerment without cost.

The engine's most durable product is the counterfeit. It delivers the experience of meaning without the cost of practice. It delivers empowerment without the risk of failure. It delivers belonging without the demand of contribution.

The counterfeit is not a fraud. It is a circuit. It closes when you feel something, imagine it came from somewhere meaningful, and stop there. The third check — the runnable instrument, the failed run, the verified protocol — never appears. The circuit stays closed. The practitioner never emerges.

The orphaning produced a population hungry for the third check. The engine produced a population satisfied with the closed circuit. The struggle between them is the struggle of the series.

### Part III — The Planes
*(The nested programming — Three Circles as nested scales)*

#### Chapter 11 — The Body Plane

Conditioning, charge, autopilot.

The body is the first circle. It runs on conditioning. It stores charge. It defaults to autopilot when the charge gets too high to hold consciously.

T1a programs the body through spectacle — attention patterns that bypass conscious choice. T1b programs the body through family habit — rhythms that run without being named. T2 untying begins at the body: noticing the autopilot, feeling the charge, choosing a response that is not the default.

The body is not a machine. It is a field. It responds to attention, to rhythm, to relationship. The domestication of the body is the domestication of attention. The untying of the body is the recovery of attention as a faculty, not a resource to be extracted.

#### Chapter 12 — The Family Plane

Tether, thread, release.

The family is the second circle. It runs on tether and thread logic. Tether is the demand that family members stay connected regardless of cost. Thread is the demand that connection be proven through obligation, sacrifice, or suffering.

T1a programs the family through spectacle — family as brand, family as consumption unit, family as identity marker. T1b programs the family through inherited habit — the rhythms of claiming, withholding, and enforcing that were set by the orphaning. T2 untying at the family scale is the recovery of thread without tether: connection that is chosen, not demanded; obligation that is offered, not extracted.

The family wound is not that family is bad. It is that family was turned into an engine. The untying is not the destruction of family. It is the restoration of family as a practice of mutual release.

#### Chapter 13 — The Civilization Plane

Registry, spectacle, control.

Civilization is the third circle. It runs on registry logic — the demand that everything be classified, filed, and administrable. It runs on spectacle — the extraction of attention as the primary resource. It runs on control — the management of both the registry and the spectacle through institutions that claim to serve the population they administer.

T1a at civilization scale is the full operation of the engine. T1b at civilization scale is the individual's internalization of the engine's logic: the belief that worth is earned, that safety requires classification, that belonging requires usefulness. T2 at civilization scale is the bridging capacity operating at the level of institutions and crowds — bird-tribe dynamics, compact-as-conduct, the crowd redistributing without a commander.

Civilization is not the problem. The problem is the engine that runs civilization as a closed system. The untying is not the destruction of civilization. It is the restoration of civilization as a practice of mutual aid rather than a mechanism of control.

#### Chapter 14 — The Maker Plane

Study, crash, absence, the gap they left.

The maker plane is the fourth scale. It is not a metaphysical realm. It is the scale at which the study operates: the makers' instruments, their protocols, their capacity to observe systems without being captured by them.

The crash was unintentional. The absence that followed was not a test. It was a loss. The makers did not leave a message. They left instruments. They left protocols. They left a gap where their regular visitation used to be.

The gap is the most important thing they left. The orphaning wound — the sense that handlers abandon you, that belonging is conditional, that authority is unreliable — is the population's response to the maker's absence. The engine is the institutional response: someone must fill the gap. The counterfeit is the spiritual response: the gap is proof that something better exists.

The practice is the third response: the gap is where you start. The bridging capacity is the capacity to operate in the gap without filling it with a fantasy of return.

#### Chapter 15 — The Intermediate Species

The bridging capacity as origin story.

The intermediate species is not a new species. It is the capacity that was latent in the domesticated line from the beginning — the capacity to bridge scales, to operate inside systems without being captured by them, to practice untying at every circle simultaneously.

The orphaning was designed to produce this capacity. It failed in the short term and succeeded in the long term. The failure produced the wound. The success produced the bridging capacity. Both are present in every person who can feel the engine running and choose a different response.

The intermediate species is not a biological category. It is a functional one: anyone who can operate T2 in the gap between T1a and T1b is an intermediate species. The novels dramatize this capacity in Eli, Wren, Somchai, and the bird-tribe cohorts. This companion explains the origin of the capacity and the mechanism of its production.

### Part IV — The Practice
*(What the orphaning created and what survives it — T2 at every scale)*

#### Chapter 16 — Manual Override

Body-scale untying.

T2 at the body circle is the practice of noticing autopilot and choosing differently. It is not a technique. It is a relationship with your own attention.

The domestication of the body is complete in most people by adulthood. The body runs on charge patterns it did not choose. It responds to spectacle it did not consent to. It holds tension it cannot name. Manual Override is the practice of recovering the capacity to name the charge, feel the tension, and choose a response that is not the default.

The practice has three components:
1. **Noticing:** the moment before autopilot runs — the pause where choice is still possible.
2. **Holding:** the charge without discharging it — the body's capacity to contain intensity without acting it out.
3. **Choosing:** the response that is not the default — the action that the system did not program.

Manual Override is not control. It is the opposite of control: it is the recovery of responsiveness. Control is autopilot with a supervisor. Manual Override is the supervisor who notices that the autopilot is running and chooses to intervene.

#### Chapter 17 — Release

Family-scale untying.

T2 at the family circle is the practice of releasing tether without destroying thread. It is not leaving family. It is refusing to let family be an engine.

The family wound is the demand that love be proven through sacrifice, that belonging be earned through obedience, that care be demonstrated through suffering. The orphaning produced families that operate on these demands because the extraction protocol required them. The engine reinforced them because spectacle and control need families to operate as consumption units and identity brands.

Release is the practice of offering thread without demanding tether: connection that does not require proof, belonging that does not require performance, care that does not require cost. This is not easy. The engine punishes release. The family that stops operating as an engine looks like a family that is falling apart to everyone who is still running engine logic.

Release has three components:
1. **Naming the engine:** seeing the spectacle-and-control logic in the family's own habits without blaming the family for operating it.
2. **Refusing the tether:** choosing connection without demanding proof, staying without requiring sacrifice.
3. **Holding the gap:** surviving the period when the family is reorganizing around a different logic — the chaos before the new rhythm settles.

#### Chapter 18 — The Compact

Civilization-scale untying.

T2 at the civilization circle is the practice of building institutions that refuse completion. It is the compact: bounded, revocable, consent-bound coordination that operates without command, without spectacle, and without the demand that everyone be useful before they are safe.

The engine produces institutions that seek permanence. The registry seeks to be universal. The spectacle seeks to be total. The control system seeks to be seamless. The compact is the opposite: it is designed to expire, to be revised, to be revoked by the people it serves.

The compact has three components:
1. **Bounded scope:** the compact does exactly what it was designed to do and nothing more.
2. **Revocable membership:** participation is voluntary, exit is unpunished, and the compact does not claim authority over non-members.
3. **Consent-bound coordination:** decisions are made by the people affected, not by the people who benefit from the decision being made.

The compact is not a document. It is a practice. It appears as conduct, not as text. The bird-tribe proof beats dramatize this: the crowd redistributing without a commander, households aligning on shared routes under pressure, the Eagle–Condor agreement functioning as a way of moving together rather than a signed treaty.

#### Chapter 19 — The Bird Tribes

Flocking without command.

Bird tribes are the civilization-scale image of T2. They are what happens when a crowd reorganizes by shared attention rather than by command.

Flocking without command is not chaos. It is a higher order of coordination. The flock moves as a single organism without a leader because each bird is attending to its neighbors' movements and adjusting its own in real time. The pattern emerges from the interaction, not from the instruction.

The engine cannot produce bird-tribe dynamics. The engine requires a commander, a spectacle, a registry. It cannot tolerate the emergence of a pattern it did not authorize. The bird tribes are the engine's nightmare: a crowd that reorganizes without permission, a civic body that distributes resources without a ledger, a community that aligns on shared routes without a central plan.

The novels show bird-tribe dynamics in three locked proof beats:
1. **Crowd redistribution without commander** (Ch 1, Brazilian harbor): under stress, people redistribute space, water, and triage priority by noticing who needs what and adjusting — no one is in charge, no one is waiting for orders.
2. **Households aligning on shared routes under pressure** (Ch 3, coastal fishing cohort + clinic cooperative): multiple households reroute supplies and shelter capacity without a central coordinator; the alignment is visible in the logistics, not in a meeting.
3. **Compact appearing as conduct, not document** (Ch 6, Brazilian assembly → Hawaiʻi invitation): the Eagle–Condor agreement functions as a way of moving together, not as a signed treaty; the conduct is the compact.

Bird tribes are not anarchy. They are a higher order of organization that the engine cannot recognize because it has no vocabulary for emergence. The engine sees only the absence of command and calls it chaos. The practitioner sees the pattern emerging and calls it practice.

#### Chapter 20 — The Court of Threads

Care work as governance without ownership.

The Court of Threads is the final image of the companion: care work as governance without ownership. It is what happens when the family plane, the civilization plane, and the maker plane all meet in a single practice.

The court is not a building. It is a procedure. It is the practice of holding people accountable without owning them, of providing care without demanding gratitude, of making decisions that affect the community without claiming authority over the community.

The court has three components:
1. **Witnessing:** every decision is witnessed by the people affected, not by the people who benefit.
2. **Review:** every decision is revisable, not because the decision was wrong but because the conditions that produced it may have changed.
3. **Release:** every decision includes the option to exit without punishment, because governance without ownership means the governed are not property.

The Court of Threads is the novel's image of what civilization could look like if it were run as a practice rather than an engine. It is not utopian. It is workable. The novels show it functioning in the cloister founding in Ch 9 of Book III: Owen's rule, the men who state they will leave if it doesn't pass, the room that is fixed before it is named. That is the Court of Threads in miniature.
