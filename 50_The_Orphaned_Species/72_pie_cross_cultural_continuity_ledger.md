> **Purpose — LOCKED:** This file is the single source of truth for the
> reconstructed roots, cognate constellations, and non-linguistic cultural
> rhymes that Maren's field notebook carries across Books I–III. It turns the
> series’ continuity goal into an auditable assignment: European, South Asian,
> East Asian, and worldwide reflexes each have a visible place in the notebook,
> and each recurrence changes meaning rather than repeating as a franchise
> token.
>
> **Register:** planning / bible. Run this prose past `14` §

> saving.

## Rules

1. **One root, many witnesses.** A PIE root belongs to the notebook because
   it appears in a demonstrable reflex in at least one on-page witness
   language. The reconstruction is scholarly scaffolding; the reader meets the
   witnesses first.
2. **Four-layer visibility.** Every continuity thread should be traceable to
   at least one European, one South Asian, one East Asian/trans-Eurasian, and
   one worldwide or non-Indo-European expression, unless a layer is genuinely
   absent from the record.
3. **Meaning changes on reuse.** A root or motif may recur across I → II → III
   only when the later book gives it a new narrative function. Reuse that
   repeats a spent meaning is cut.
4. **Notebook as mesh, not lecture.** Continuity lands through Maren's
   comparative cipher, a marginal gloss, a living community's correction, or
   Eli's bodily test — not through a character explaining cosmology to Eli.
5. **Sanxingdui stays outside the language tree.** Sanxingdui material grammar
   rhymes with notebook structure; it is never offered as PIE, IE, or
   universal-language proof. See `52` § *The Sanxingdui break*.
6. **Contested reconstructions remain contested.** Exact PIE forms, sound
   correspondences, and spellings require specialist review before manuscript
   prose. The novel presents the constellation; the scholarly apparatus does
   not win an argument the book does not stage.

## Craft checks

- No notebook page only explains lore. Decoding always changes a route,
  relationship, experiment, or choice.
- The reader must see enough recurring forms to attempt the relationship
  before each answer lands.
- Maren is not omniscient. One error harms the route, one correction changes
  Eli's view of her, and one contested page keeps a subject visible.
- Non-Indo-European comparisons strain the single-language-tree model in Book
  II; Book III widens the mesh without erasing the strain.
