# Bridging-Capacity Gestures — Three-Circle Crossings Across Four Volumes

**Purpose:** enumerate the gestures by which the series demonstrates intermediate-species T2 capacity: moments when the body circle, family circle, and civilization circle are not merely co-present but actively held in relation. Each gesture must cross at least two circle boundaries; the series arc escalates from one crossing per gesture in Vol I to all three circles in Vol IV.
**Status:** LOCKED scaffold, 2026-08-05.

---

## Circle Map

|---|---|---|---|---|

| Family / middle | shared life, household, Weavers' Net, chosen kin | marriage category, house servitude, assigned role, Extra insult | Wren/Rowan bond, foster placement, Somchai's clinic, threshold-house practice | consent-bound aid, repair, witness, accountable disentanglement |

**Key:** T1 = inherited program. T2 = what you do in the gap between program and impulse. The gesture proves T2 capacity by showing the body circle and the family circle and the civilization circle simultaneously, with no term missing.

## Vol I — *The Orphaned Species* — One-Circle Crossings

**Arc task:** establish that T1a, T1b, and T2 each exist as a separate layer; no gesture yet holds all three.

### Gesture 1 — Body→Family
**Chapter:** Eli first accepts Wren's presence in the cabin.
**What crosses:** Eli's body-level hypervigilance (T1a: orphan-drift as survival habit) meets Wren's chosen-kin offer (T1b: foster/Weaver lineage care) without collapsing into romance. He sleeps in the same room and does not touch her.
**Check:** the scene names the body fear and the chosen-kin offer in the same beat. Neither term wins alone.

### Gesture 2 — Family→Civilization
**Chapter:** Mei's kitchen scene / Brazilian intake threshold house.
**What crosses:** household warmth (T1b: hearth-as-belonging) meets registry logic (T1a: Charkha claim routing). The woman who feeds Eli does not hand him over; she names the difference between feeding and filing.
**Check:** the scene shows the two vocabularies active in the same room. The reader sees that the household is not naive; it is political.

### Gesture 3 — Body→Civilization
**Chapter:** Eli's first encounter with the Union registry / Maren's file.
**What crosses:** Eli's somatic signatures (T2: his actual felt body) meet the Union's clinical file (T1a: assigned sector / claim category). The file calls him psion; his body calls itself hungry and tired.
**Check:** the body read contradicts the file read. The reader must feel that the body is the more reliable source, without the book saying so explicitly.

## Vol II — *The Descent* — Two-Circle Crossings

**Arc task:** two circles held at once; the gap between them becomes visible.

### Gesture 4 — Body→Family→Civilization (first three-circle attempt, incomplete)
**Chapter:** Somchai's healer refusal / clinic scene.
**What crosses:** Somchai's somatic practice (T2: body) runs through his clinic's chosen-kin network (T1b: shared care labor) inside a Mandate-claimed zone (T1a: scientist claiming). He heals Eli without sedating him, refuses the registry lane, and does so inside the very apparatus that claims both of them.
**Check:** the scene shows T2 surviving inside T1a territory. The cost is not freedom — Somchai remains claimed. But the body practice is intact.

### Gesture 5 — Family→Civilization
**Chapter:** Wren/Rowan reunion at the Extra settlement.
**What crosses:** Wren's Weaver knotting (T1b: formed bond, formative relation) meets Rowan's Extra demobilized identity (T1a: settlement category). Their reconciliation does not erase the category; it names it and moves through it. Rowan is still Extra on paper; Wren is still a Knotter inside institutions.
**Check:** the scene shows the bond surviving the category without pretending the category does not exist.

### Gesture 6 — Body→Civilization (intensified)
**Chapter:** Eli's corridor synchronization refusal / Pömmelte purge geometry.
**What crosses:** Eli's Manual Override capacity (T2: body) meets Sol's forced-synchronization offer (T1a: civilization-scale god-sickness). He refuses on the record; the refusal costs a west-bridge corridor falling before enough voluntary replies arrive.
**Check:** T2 refusal is public and costly. It is not wisdom in a safe room; it is a decision that spends real people.

## Vol III — *The Living World* — Three-Circle Gestures

**Arc task:** all three circles held simultaneously; the compact is the social form that makes this legible.

### Gesture 7 — Three-Circle Compact (first civic form)
**Chapter:** Ch 6 — The Compact.
**What crosses:** the compact's terms (civilization layer: T1a/T2 hybrid) require embodied account, relational account, and technical account for every major decision — forcing the body circle (T2), family/community circle (T1b), and governance circle (T1a) into the same record.
**Check:** the Three Circles are not a metaphor in this scene; they are procedural. A decision without all three registers is incomplete.

### Gesture 8 — Bird-Tribe Proof Beat 1 (body→family→civilization)
**Chapter:** Ch 1 — Brazilian harbor intake.
**What crosses:** exhausted port coordinators redistribute water, triage, and interpreter duty by shared attention (T2: body-observed need) rather than hierarchy (T1a). The redistribution is faster and safer than the commander-led version would have been.
**Check:** no character receives credit or promotion. The reader sees the shift through handoffs and gaps filled before anyone asks.

### Gesture 9 — Bird-Tribe Proof Beat 2 (family→civilization, body implied)
**Chapter:** Ch 3 — evacuation corridor alignment.
**What crosses:** multiple households independently name their own conditions (T1b) and the route holds only because the conditions overlap (T2 coordination without commander; T1a evac logic overridden by community consent).
**Check:** Sol's forces and Lang's Standard are both trying to force the same route. The communities agree precisely by refusing both commands.

### Gesture 10 — Bird-Tribe Proof Beat 3 (civilization→body)
**Chapter:** Ch 6 — shelter allocation dispute.
**What crosses:** a Brazilian host and a Union officer follow the compact's terms without consulting the document (T2 practice already internalized). When the only signed copy burns, nobody panics.
**Check:** civic order lives in conduct, not paper. The body-circle proof is that the community does not reach for the document; the conduct is already running.

### Gesture 11 — Body→Civilization (maximum pressure)
**Chapter:** Ch 8 — The Cost of Consent.
**What crosses:** Eli refuses forced synchronization at a distant corridor under live temptation (T2 body refusal) while the compact's bounded authority (civilization T1a/T2 hybrid) absorbs the cost — Tomas dies; the threshold house is destroyed.
**Check:** refusal without shame. Eli does not hand the community to Lang or Sol. The compact survives incompletely.

### Gesture 12 — Family→Civilization (cloister founding)
**Chapter:** Ch 9 — Cloister founding hearing.
**What crosses:** Extra men, veterans, Harbor Thread elders, and Eli co-found the first cloisters (T1b: chosen kin / mentorship lineage) without the Weavers' blessing and without Lang's classification (T1a: registry avoidance). The men gain kept standing through attention, repair, watch, care, restraint, and accountable service.
**Check:** a visible male body that is neither house servitude nor Thread placement nor certificate of romantic acceptability. The gesture is civic; the method is familial.

## Vol IV — *The Court of Threads* — All Three Circles Sustained

**Arc task:** all three circles are not only held simultaneously but tested under cross-examination. The court is the civic form that requires each register to answer to the others.

### Gesture 13 — Three-Circle Court
**Chapter:** Court proceedings across all chapters.
**What crosses:** the court requires embodied account (T2), relational account (T1b), and technical/provenance account (T1a) for every contested claim. No single register can decide alone. The court is the compact's procedural descendant made formal.
**Check:** the court's rules are the Three Circles written as law.

### Gesture 14 — Body→Family→Civilization (full release)
**Chapter:** Ch 9 — The Room Prepared / Ch 10 — The Threshold Crossed.
**What crosses:** Eli completes Manual Override at body circle (T2), chooses Wren at family circle (T1b), and submits his conduct to ordinary public inquiry at civilization circle (T1a). He does not renounce power; he uses it correctly and releases it.
**Check:** not renunciation — correct use, release, and review. The three circles are separate and all answered.
