## Chapter 24—The Sideways Four Seconds
The research room is a converted closet in the southern outpost. It has a table, two chairs, a shelf with field notebooks, and a recorder that does not record anything except the time. The recorder is analog. The hand is second. The face is scratched.

Somchai sits at the table. He is alone. He has been alone for three hours. The other researchers have left. They went to the clinic to watch the child's first motor milestone. The child is seven months old. The milestone is reaching. The child reached for a toy and missed. The toy was a wooden horse with one leg missing.

Somchai did not go to the clinic. He stayed in the closet with the recorder. He is trying to reproduce the four seconds.

The four seconds happened three months ago. They happened during a shift change. Somchai was carrying a bag of medical supplies from the west gate to the clinic. The bag was heavy. The corridor was steep. He stopped to rest. He leaned against the wall. He closed his eyes.

For four seconds, the corridor was not the corridor. The wall was not the wall. The bag was not heavy. The time between his breath in and his breath out was not time. It was a sideways thing. It had a shape. It had a direction. It had a name that was not a name.

He opened his eyes. The corridor was the corridor. The wall was the wall. The bag was heavy. The time was normal. He continued walking. He did not mention it to anyone.

The researchers found out three days later. They had been monitoring the field signatures. They saw the dip. They asked him about it. He told them. They asked him to reproduce it. He tried. He could not.

They have been trying since. They have tried different corridors. Different walls. Different bags. Different times of day. They have tried with music. They have tried with silence. They have tried with the child present. The child was asleep. The four seconds did not happen.

Somchai is not a researcher. He is a field medic. He does not have the vocabulary for what the four seconds were. He does not have the instruments to measure them. He has the body that carried them.

He sits at the table. He closes his eyes. He leans back. He breathes in. He breathes out. He waits.

Nothing happens.

He opens his eyes. The recorder ticks. The hand moves. The second is ordinary. The corridor is ordinary. The bag is ordinary.

He writes in the field notebook. He writes: "The four seconds are not reproducible. They answered back once and then collapsed. I do not know what they were. I do not know what they wanted."

He closes the notebook. He puts it on the shelf. He stands. He picks up the bag. He walks to the clinic.

The child is still reaching. The toy is still on the floor. The child has not reached it yet. The child's mother is sitting beside the toy. She is not helping. She is waiting.

Somchai stops. He watches. The child reaches again. The hand opens. The fingers close. The toy is touched. The child does not pick it up. The child feels it.

The mother looks at Somchai. She does not smile. She says, "He is not in a hurry."

Somchai nods. He says, "I know."

He walks to the ward. He has work to do. The clinic needs saline. The corridor needs a shift change. The bag needs to be carried.

The researchers will keep looking. They will keep monitoring. They will keep asking him to reproduce it. He will keep trying. He will keep failing. The four seconds will remain sideways. They will remain ordinary. They will not be prophecy. They will not be healing. They will be a bag, a shift, it ends.

Tao 63: do your work, then step back. The only path to serenity. The four seconds are not prophecy. They are the moment before labor. Ordinary and unnamed.

John 9:1-3: neither this man nor his parents sinned. This happened so that the works of God might be displayed. Somchai refuses to read the four seconds as divine. They are just labor.

The recorder ticks. The hand moves. The second is ordinary. The corridor is ordinary. The bag is ordinary. Somchai walks to the ward.
