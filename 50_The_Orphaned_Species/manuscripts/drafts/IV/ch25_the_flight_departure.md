## Chapter 25—The Flight Departure
The apprentices leave before dawn. They do not say goodbye. They do not gather. They do not make a speech. They just pack their bags and walk to the gate.

The bags are small. Each one contains a tool, a seed packet, and a song. The tool is ordinary: a wrench, a spade, a saw, a needle, a length of rope. The seed packet is labeled by hand. The song is not written down. It is the work-song from the gutter, from the field, from the corridor. It has no words. It has rhythm and breath.

The court has already drafted a statement. It calls them a movement. It calls them evidence of portable discipline. It calls them the future of the compact. The Weavers have already drafted a different statement. It calls them a lineage. It calls them proof that route discipline travels. It calls them a precedent.

The apprentices read neither statement. They do not know the statements exist. They are too busy checking their bags.

There are nine of them. Five are from the corridor crew. Two are from the clinic. One is from the garden. One is from the work-song class. The youngest is sixteen. The oldest is twenty-one. None of them have names that will survive the year.

The gate officer is Rico. He is the same Rico who stood at the medical gate three days before the drone strike. He is older now. He has a scar on his left hand from a burn he got fighting the shed fire. He checks their papers. He does not ask where they are going. He does not ask why. He just checks the papers and opens the gate.

The first apprentice steps through. She is holding a wrench. She has a seed packet in her pocket. She is humming the work-song under her breath. She does not look back.

The second apprentice steps through. He is holding a spade. He has a seed packet in his pocket. He is humming the work-song under his breath. He does not look back.

The third apprentice steps through. She is holding a needle. She has a seed packet in her pocket. She is humming the work-song under her breath. She does not look back.

The fourth apprentice steps through. He is holding a length of rope. He has a seed packet in his pocket. He is humming the work-song under his breath. He does not look back.

The fifth apprentice steps through. She is holding a saw. She has a seed packet in her pocket. She is humming the work-song under her breath. She does not look back.

The sixth apprentice steps through. He is from the clinic. He is holding a medical kit. He has a seed packet in his pocket. He is humming the work-song under his breath. He does not look back.

The seventh apprentice steps through. She is from the garden. She is holding a trowel. She has a seed packet in her pocket. She is humming the work-song under her breath. She does not look back.

The eighth apprentice steps through. He is from the work-song class. He is holding a drum. He has a seed packet in his pocket. He is humming the work-song under his breath. He does not look back.

The ninth apprentice steps through. She is the youngest. She is holding a fastener. It is the same fastener she sorted in the gutter on the first day. She has a seed packet in her pocket. She is humming the work-song under her breath. She does not look back.

Rico closes the gate. He locks it. He puts the key in his pocket. He does not write anything in the log. He does not call anyone. He just stands there and listens to the work-song recede.

Eli is not at the gate. He is in the chamber. He is signing papers. The papers are transfer documents. The transfer is complete. The compact is certified. The handover is done. The hunger is quiet.

Malia finds him. She is holding a note. The note is from the southern outpost. It says the rotating site network functioned without him for a full season. It says the corridors are open. It says the clinics are staffed. It says the gate at the southern boundary is manned by a seventeen-year-old who does not ask questions.

Eli reads the note. He does not say anything. He folds it. He puts it in his pocket.

Wren finds him. She is holding a different note. It is shorter. It says only: discovered, not pursued. She does not explain. She does not need to. Eli understands.

He understands that the next generation is already moving. He understands that they are not a movement. They are not a precedent. They are not a lineage. They are just people with tools and seeds and songs, walking through a gate that was opened for them.

Tao 32: The Tao is forever undefined. Though small in the world, nothing in the world can make it serve. The apprentices are small. They are not served by myth.

Matthew 10:16: I am sending you out like sheep among wolves. Therefore be as shrewd as snakes and as innocent as doves. They leave carrying songs, not swords.

Revelation 22:13: I am the Alpha and the Omega, the First and the Last, the Beginning and the End. The apprentices refuse to be the beginning or the end.

The gate closes. The work-song recedes. The seed packets travel. The next generation inherits them as precedent without the protection of a founder. The rotating site network functions without Eli for a full season. The compact holds. The child reaches. The toy is touched.

The book ends with a gate closing and a song walking away. No myth. No founder. No return.
