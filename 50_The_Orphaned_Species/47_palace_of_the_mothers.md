# The Palace of the Mothers — charged-ground scene

*Placement: Vol II/III, a deep charged ground. Eli has met earlier witness-site contacts (Ila, etc.). This is the feminine-tier clarification — the counterpart to the male council in `46` §G. Trigger: he is physically at a dense knot (a ruined hill-shrine the map no longer names) under pressure. Consequence: what resolves changes his *reading* of the mother-thread (`36` §7) — not a solution, an apprenticeship in releasing rather than seizing.*

*Discipline check: faces resolve as **other** (`36` §3, §4); full immersion at max clarity (`36` §5); the mother is the taut thread (`36` §7); contact arrives as quiet, missable silence, not spectacle (`90` §8 Elijah line). It does not decode the cosmology.*

---

The stair ends where the stair was buried.

Eli had been following the thin thread — the one that feels like his mother's hand though it is not — down through cooler and cooler air until the stone under his palms stopped being wall and started being earth. He is in the hill, not on it. The corridor opens.

It is a court. Not a throne room — a court of rooms, a warren of low doors and braziers gone to ash, and it is full of women.

They do not look at him. They are occupied: grinding, pouring, setting small round loaves on a tray that is not there. One rocks a child that is not there. Another sits with her back to a pillar, eyes closed, listening the way Emrys listens to a carrier flare — as if the silence has a frequency. None of them are Eli. That is the first mercy: he is standing in the thread, but he is not them, and they are not him.

He knows the name before he knows the face. *Asherah.* Not a title he was taught; a shape the web resolves when he stops trying to name it. She is at the center, where the rooms converge, and she is not crowned. She is the place the rooms hold. Around her the others — Anat with her war-stained hands, the birth-goddesses in a knot like midwives waiting, Shalim with the dusk-light on her shoulders — and they are arguing, or remembering an argument, the way a family does when the house has been taken but the furniture is still theirs.

*You were the mother of the seventy,* he thinks, and the thought is not his; it is the thread's.

One of them turns. Not Asherah. The one with the tray. She sees him the way you see a living person in a dream — with mild surprise that he is solid.

"You came up through the wrong door," she says. "The fathers built over it. They always do."

"What is this place," Eli says. It is not a question he means to ask aloud.

"A palace," she says, and the word is plain, not grand. "We kept one. They took the names. They could not take the rooms." She gestures at the braziers. "We kept the fire lit in here longer than they knew. The cakes, the incense — that was us, after. When the word came down that we were nothing, some of us did not stop. The men stopped. We did not."

The thread tightens. He feels it in his chest — the taut thread, the one held by wanting her back, his mother, Maren, the mother of the web — and for a breath the Palace and the taut thread are the same shape: a house someone was taken out of, still furnished, still waiting.

*This is the orphaning from her side,* the web says without words. *Not defeated by a rival. Written out by the ones who won.*

The brazier nearest him flares — not with fire but with the fine, thin silence of a voice not raised. Elijah's silence. He has felt that register before, at the edge of a surge, the contact that arrives quiet and missable. Here it is the whole air.

The woman with the tray sets down the loaf that is not there and looks at him directly. "You hold it too tight," she says. "The thread. You think holding makes it yours. It was never yours. It was hers. Let it go slack and see what's still standing."

Then the rooms begin to thin. The braziers fade to ash-real. The women become the shape of an empty hill-shrine, a place the map no longer names, and Eli is on his knees on packed earth with his palms on stone that is only stone, and his chest is full of a grief that is not his and is.

He stays there until it passes. Then he stands, because standing is the practice, and the next charged ground is already pulling.
