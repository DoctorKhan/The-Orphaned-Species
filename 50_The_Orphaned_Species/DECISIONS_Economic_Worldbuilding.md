# Economic Worldbuilding — Systemic Labor and Credential Mismatch

> **LOCKED 2026-08-09.** Companion to `51_field_sail_and_mandate_peace.md`, `05_story_architecture.md`, `99_open_questions.md`, and Book III beatsheet. No new plot moves.

## Governing principle

Economic domination operates through **conditional access**, not open conquest. The Mandate does not need to own a territory to own its labor. It controls ports, fuel, medicine, credit, transit, and reconstruction contracts, then converts ordinary survival choices into extraction channels. The result is not slavery in name, but dependency so tight that refusal is functionally impossible.

## Two locked systemic issues

### 1. Labor export via economic domination

**Mechanism:** The Mandate issues work permits, reconstruction contracts, and "humanitarian labor placements" that bind displaced people to Mandate-managed sites — ports, farms, field camps, logistics corridors. Workers are paid in credits usable only inside Mandate-controlled markets; transit home depends on clearance the Mandate can withhold. Contract terms include mandatory deductions for shelter, transport, and "processing," leaving earnings below subsistence. Complaints are routed through Mandate-administered tribunals with long backlogs and no independent appeal.

**On-page instances:**
- **Book III harbor intake:** newly awakened refugees receive intake forms that classify them as labor-ready before they receive medical clearance. The form Eli signs authorizes placement; the placement office routes the able-bodied to dock repair and food-packing shifts before family reunification is processed.
- **Brazilian river-port network:** Mandate-backed contractors run harvest and river-transport labor under "joint civilian recovery" banners. Local cooperatives that refuse the terms lose credit access and find their produce blocked from export corridors.
- **San Francisco command center (Book III):** the Union’s own emergency logistics depend on labor pools the Mandate supplies under the ceasefire’s civilian cooperation clause — dependency dressed as aid.

**Narrative function:** this is the economic arm of the coercion Eli is already fighting militarily. It does not need a new scene; it shows up in the paperwork he signs, the manifest Mei keeps, the worker roster Hassan reads, and the conversation where Commissioner Ma explains why the corridor is open and the port still isn’t.

### 2. Credential/job mismatch — educated surplus, survival labor

**Mechanism:** war and displacement destroy the credential-economy correspondence. Doctors drive food trucks; lawyers process intake manifests; physicists work dock repair. The mismatch is not comic; it is structural. The Mandate and remnant states recognize only current clearance, not prior qualification, because recognition creates obligation. Keeping educated populations uncredentialed makes them replaceable and mobile.

**On-page instances:**
- **Book III constituent assembly / hearing:** witnesses with advanced degrees testify about events in a room that treats their statements as equivalent to any other survivor account. Their training gives them forensic habits the inquiry needs, but the system does not name that skill as qualification.
- **Wren’s placement desk:** her network already trades on credential verification and status transfer. The mismatch shows when her manifest system logs a former surgeon as "general labor" because no current license is recognized.
- **Somchai’s clinic network:** medically trained refugees work as orderlies and supply runners because licensing is jurisdiction-bound and the clinic operates across three overlapping legal zones.

**Narrative function:** this is what Book III’s "coordination under pressure" actually coordinates. The talent is present; the social form to name and use it is not.

## Prose rules

1. Show economic pressure through **paperwork, queues, and routing decisions**, not lectures.
2. Labor export is visible in the forms Eli signs, the worker lists Hassan reads, the clinic intake categories Mei questions, and the port rosters Wren audits.
3. Credential mismatch appears in the gap between what a character knows and what the system lets them do — a retired cartographer reading river charts for a contractor, a former actuary rebuilding a clinic’s supply model in a notebook.
4. The Mandate is not the only actor. Local cooperatives, Union administrators, and Free Port operators all make choices that reproduce dependency in smaller register.
5. No new plot moves. These mechanisms run inside existing scenes and pressures.
