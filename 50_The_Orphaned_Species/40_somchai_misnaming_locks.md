# Somchai — Perpetual Misnaming Locks: *bene elohim* / *bene apkallu* / *elohim*

> **Status:** LOCKED 2026-08-21
> **Companion to:** `26_present_day_cast_expanded.md`; `32_volume_II_beatsheet.md`; `33_volume_III_beatsheet.md`; `60_volume_IV_court_of_threads.md`; `35_artifact_chain.md`; `45_divine_names.md`; `54_litanies.md`
> **Governing principle:** Somchai is never divine, never psionic, never a vessel. The misnaming is always someone else’s upgrade. His job is refusal, not revelation.

## Why this thread is allowed — LOCKED

The series already plants three parallel misnaming pressures:
1. **Deiwos / sons of Deiwos** — applied to Eli, not Somchai, but in the same register of folk grammar upgrading a person into a sky-father slot (`52` § *Son of Deiwos*).
2. **Phantom god-perception** — the series’ locked domestication thesis: perceiving gods who are not there so service continues after handlers leave. Book II seeds the felt pattern; Book III makes it a civic diagnosis (`17` §9a; `40`).
3. **Apkallu / *bene elohim* / Igigi / Watchers** — the deep-time material already names the children of the gods as a people, not a title (`35`; `45`; `00_MASTER_TIMELINE.md`).

Somchai’s misnaming thread uses all three without collapsing them. The folk do not know the apkallu lore. They reach for the words they have: *healer*, *saint*, *bene elohim*, *elohim*. Each upgrade is a category error. Each is also a political attempt — to own him, credit him, or recruit him.

---

## Book II — *The Descent*: healers get named, not upgraded yet

**Locked form:** folk labels only, not the deep-time terms.

**Appearances:**
- After Somchai’s public healing at the Thrace clinic (Ch 4), patients and pilgrims call him *healer* in the register of gratitude, not theology. Some call him *saint* or *teacher*. One old woman calls him *father* because the clinic is the only place that didn’t turn her away.
- At Pömmelte / broken-line church (Ch 6), a survivor tells Eli the ground healed her; Eli knows it was Somchai’s triage kit and a borrowed antibiotic. The ground did not do it. The misnaming is already here: the event is credited to the place, not the person, but the shape is the same.

**Somchai’s response:** he re-labels himself into the ordinary. *Nurse. Field medic. I have a bag and a shift ends.* No sermon. No correction that sounds like a confession. He refuses the healer identity in Book I already; Book II deepens the pressure without converting it.

**Prose guardrail:** no character says *bene elohim* or *apkallu* in Book II. The theological vocabulary is still underground.

---

## Book III — *The Compact*: first theological upgrade attempt

**Locked form:** a local tries to upgrade him; Somchai refuses in conduct, not theology.

**Appearance:** during the Brazilian harbor / clinic intake sequence (Ch 1–2), a refugee or local elder witnesses Somchai stabilize someone under pressure. The elder says, in street register, something like: *you are not a man doing a job. You are elohim among us.* Maybe softer: *bene elohim* — one sent, not born here.

**Somchai’s response:** he does not argue. He keeps working. If the elder insists, Somchai names the cost: *If I am elohim, then every person who died while I was setting up the line is my failure. I am not carrying that.* He reframes the claim as ownership with better clothes. The elder does not back down; the reader sees that belief survives refutation.

**Political angle:** Lang or a Continuity officer later notes that Somchai’s public reputation is a recruitment vulnerability. They do not try to crown him; they try to file him. The misnaming is useful because it makes a witness tractable.

**Prose guardrail:** the *elohim* or *bene elohim* line is spoken by a non-Somchai character, in a local register. Somchai never uses the term about himself. The scene ends with him still working, not posing.

---

## Book IV — *The Court of Threads*: faction attempts to own the title

**Locked form:** others argue over his classification; Somchai declines the entire category.

**Appearance:** during the Hollow Year crisis, a cloister, threshold house, or court faction drafts Somchai into a theological argument. They cite the healings, the publics, the chalked shelters, the dream-pilots, and the twins rumor. They say he is *bene apkallu* — the teaching line, not the ruling line; the snake, not the bird; the repair caste, not the watch.

**Why *bene apkallu*:** this is the one label that sounds like a job description. Apkallu are the teaching, healing, city-foundation sages. It lets a faction argue that Somchai belongs to the compact, not above it. They are still trying to own him. They are just using a more precise vocabulary.

**Somchai’s response:** he refuses the category entirely. *Apkallu, elohim, Watcher, son — pick one. I am the man who set the IV line and held the pressure until the doctor came. That is the whole thing.* If pushed, he names the real bill: *When you call me that, the next thing you do is decide who I must answer to.* The scene’s proof is that he keeps healing after the refusal.

**Parallel:** this runs against Eli’s Deiwos crown pressure in the same book. Eli is being asked to become indispensable; Somchai is being asked to become sacred. Both refusals are load-bearing.

## Cross-book character rule — LOCKED

- Somchai never upgrades himself.
- The theological labels always come from outside: patients, pilgrims, survivors, factions, record-keepers.
- Each label is an attempt to change what someone can ask of him.
- His refusal is always bodily and practical, not doctrinal. He does not cite scripture. He cites the bag, the shift, the next patient.
- The divine-names thread is not a spiritual arc. It is a possession arc. The question is not *is he divine?* The question is *who gets to decide what a healer is worth?*

---

## Prose guardrails

- No scene in which Somchai accepts *elohim*, *bene elohim*, *bene apkallu*, Deiwos, or any cognate as accurate.
- No exposition explaining apkallu lore through Somchai’s mouth. If a character names the term, it must be a speaker who has read the journal or is using faction vocabulary.
- The apkallu / *bene elohim* distinction is theological grammar for readers, not Somchai’s self-description.
- If a scene needs him to save a life, it must still cost him something visible — exhaustion, exposure, a clinic burned, a patient he could not reach.
- If a scene ends with someone kneeling or calling him sacred and the draft leaves the kneeling uncorrected, it fails this lock.
