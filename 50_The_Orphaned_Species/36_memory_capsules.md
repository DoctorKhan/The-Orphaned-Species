# Memory Capsules — How the Deep Time Reaches the Present

*The delivery mechanism for all deep-past material in the trilogy. Resolves the "one book or two" question: the standalone Aedan-cave outline (`37_deep_time_source.md`, formerly `00_outline.md`) does **not** stay a separate book. Its content dissolves into the present-day trilogy as **memory capsules retrieved from megalithic stones.** Companion to `/00_NARRATIVE_STRUCTURE.md` (the spine), `05_story_architecture.md` (§10–11 lore discipline), `35_artifact_chain.md`, and `37_deep_time_source.md` (the harvested scene detail).*

> **Decision — LOCKED.** Deep-time material is not framed by an Aedan-in-the-cave novel and is not lectured by Emrys. It arrives diegetically, under present pressure, when Eli is at a stone/node — as imprints stored in the grid and played back through him. This is not a new mechanic: the grid already *stores high-charge imprints*, stones are the *antennae*, and Cuno already encoded a transmission into geometry, rhythm, and a marked object. "Memory capsule from a stone" only names the existing mechanic vividly. The drafted Aru chapter (`MANUSCRIPT_v1_archive.md`, the King's Men descent) is already a working example.

---

## 1. Two registers — keep them feeling different

A stone can deliver two distinct things. If both arrive in the same prose-register, the cosmology reads as a lived-life descent in costume, and the lecture the book is built to avoid sneaks back in.

| Register | What it is | POV & cadence | What it leaves behind |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Lived life** | Eli *becomes* the carrier — full embodied imprint | First-person, full sensory, present-tense inside the life; the grief of being torn back out on waking | Embodied information that still requires present practice; a person grieved, not a lesson |
| **Watched vision** | The stone *plays* a myth Eli observes — he was never these people | Myth-cadence ("dragon-cadence"), watched-not-inhabited, fragmentary, lossy | A recognition or a question, not a practice; never a clean explanation |

**The Seven are lived lives.** The cosmology (Seven Pairs, the Activation, the war, Adapa) is **watched vision** — Eli cannot descend into the Seven Pairs as a life, because no single life is there to inhabit.

## 2. The through-Aedan nesting — how the cosmology arrives lossy

The watched-vision material was originally *shown to Aedan in the cave*. In current canon Aedan is a separate Bronze-Age **partial receiver/relay**, not a frame protagonist and not one of the Seven. So:

**Eli does not watch the cosmology directly — he retrieves Aedan watching it.** A recording of a recording. The whole standalone cave-novel collapses into one deep, recursive capsule: Eli descends into Aedan, and through Aedan's eyes catches the deep-time visions, incomplete and degraded.

Why this is the right vehicle:

- **On-theme.** Transmission without ownership; the archive as palimpsest; the Cuno→Derw lossiness made structural rather than stated.
- **It justifies the fragmentation.** Third-hand reception through a partial receiver *cannot* yield a clean lecture — which is exactly the lore discipline `05` §11 demands. The cosmology arrives as shards because of how it's carried, not because the author is withholding.
- **It keeps Aedan and the cave canonical and load-bearing** without a rival protagonist. The cave, the dragons, the raid, the granary stone, the heat-migration all survive *inside* one capsule; the present-tense frame (old Parts I & IV) does not.

## 4. The firing rule (so capsules don't stall the plot)

A capsule may fire only when **both** hold:

1. **Trigger** — Eli is physically at a stone, node, buried interface, or marked object, under present pressure (pursuit, injury, a near-death threshold, a contested site). No capsule fires from a museum placard or a calm explanation.
2. **Consequence** — what it shows **changes the route, the pursuit, or a relationship in this scene.** If it only "explains the cosmology," it belongs in the bible (`37`, `00_MASTER_TIMELINE.md`), not the chapter.

Corollaries, inherited from `05` §10–11:

- No capsule provides an exact solution to the present problem. It supplies an apprenticeship or a shard, not an answer.
- Lived-life capsules cost recovery time and risk dissolution; watched-vision capsules cost certainty (they can be misread — and being third-hand through Aedan, they *should* sometimes be wrong).
- One or two unfamiliar proper terms per capsule, maximum. Role before label.
- Preserve evidence that contradicts Eli's preferred reading; let the lossiness mislead at least once.

## 5. Trigger-site anchoring

Each capsule fires at the stone where its content actually happened, so the trigger and the vision share a site. This makes the present-day location load-bearing rather than incidental.

**Aru → Göbekli Tepe — LOCKED.** The Aru capsule kicks off in Anatolia (Vol II), when Eli is at Göbekli Tepe and touches **the fox-pillar Aru carved** — the descent drops him into the carving of that very stone, with the spiral going into the fox's eye. The trigger *is* the artifact: the pillar in front of him is the one his hands are about to cut eleven thousand years down. No transit stone, no museum placard — contact with the pillar under present pressure (contested excavation / faction or military control of the site, staging TBD).

- **Draft reconciliation.** The drafted prose (`MANUSCRIPT_v1_archive.md`, Ch 7) fires Aru at the **King's Men / Rollright** in an English field during the early flight with Wren. The *descent content* (lines on Aru, the foxes, the spiral) is already Göbekli-correct and is retained. Only the *present-day frame* is mislocated and is superseded: Eli reaches this capsule at Göbekli in Vol II, not at Rollright in Vol I.
- **What the King's Men frame becomes.** Per `/00_NARRATIVE_STRUCTURE.md`, **Rollright → Albion (Leg 7, Vol III).** The drafted stone-touch frame is a reusable draft asset for a late capsule there — most naturally **Cuno** (the Bluestone Mover, Stonehenge/Albion), whose content belongs in that landscape.
