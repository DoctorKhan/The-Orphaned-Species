## Reference frame

- stakes species-level; **body stays ridiculous**;
- smart and **undignified** at once;
- physical comedy **during** problem-solving;
- buddy warmth between scared people doing careful work;
- kinetic rhythm, then one quiet beat.

**Not:** cosmology parody, quip-bro defusal, comic relief who dies first, jokes at mother-release climax.

## Distribution — LOCKED 2026-08-01 (resolves Emrys purge gap)

No single primary carrier. The full instrument-slapstick register (earnest over-explanation, undignified competence, buddy panic, wrong register at wrong time, measuring when he should touch) was built for one mentor archetype that no longer exists in the cast, and forcing all of it onto an existing character flattens someone who's doing real work elsewhere — Rasel's established love/anger/compromise, Wren's armor-break, Somchai's grief-driven vocation. Comedy distributes instead: the ensemble register already below stays as the main engine, and two existing characters take one narrow, bounded slice each — only the piece that's genuinely true to them, not the whole register on loan.

- **Rasel — narrow, post-rescue only (Book I close / Book II open).** Not goofball; one specific crack in his competence-armor: he processes love through precision because he doesn't have a more direct language for it — over-explaining coupling-instrument specs or garden chemistry at the exact moment Eli needs something less technical. Poignant-funny, a handful of beats, never an engine. Already seeded (`31` Ch 13's tomato-tied-to-lab-cuff ending image; his "chosen craft Eli cannot master").
- **Somchai — gallows/practical humor, his own scenes only (Book II).** Dark, dry, competence-adjacent field-medic humor — a real coping register for someone who does triage — distinct from the Vol I–II fellowship register below. Supports his established vulnerability-under-competence arc; never undercuts the "son of a man" gravity of his central beat.

The buddy-warmth and physical-comedy-during-problem-solving DNA that made the old register work survives through **whoever's in the room under pressure** — Eli, Wren, and situational local experts working a problem together — rather than one character performing comic relief.

## Situation comedy — misfiled by protocol — LOCKED 2026-08-06

A standing register for Books III–IV (seeded by the Cherry Cube surprise-guest beat in Book I Ch 2, which is this comedy's ancestor). The engine is always the same: **an institution files Eli under the wrong category, and the wrong category is more dignified than the man.** The comedy is situational status inversion, never quips — Eli stays the dry straight man; the institution performs the joke on itself. Every scene must also advance a clock or a relationship cost, and each scene's point-of-view job is one of: messiah projection made ridiculous, worker identity refusing the costume, or protocol forced to invent a category that doesn't exist.

**The ambassador dinner — Book III, LOCKED.** During the relocated-government negotiation window (Ch 4–6 assembly stretch), the Union delegation's protocol office misfiles Eli as a visiting head of state — the compact's signatory list has no category for *provisional coordinator*, so the roster sorts him upward by default. He is seated next to the ranking Union continuity figure at the formal meal. The scene's beats:

1. Eli arrives in what he has — work-worn, one good layer over facilities clothes; the protocol officer decides this must be deliberate diplomatic understatement.
2. Seated next to the world leader, he clocks the room the only way he knows: service routes, kitchen timing, which staff member is about to drop a tray. He catches the tray or fixes the podium cable before the toast, and the room reads it as security instinct.
3. Asked about his delegation, he answers truthfully — three people, a healer, and a boat — and the table hears it as a delegation of three ministers, a physician, and a vessel. No one corrects the record because correcting it would embarrass the protocol office.
4. The world leader speaks to him as an equal and Eli answers as a worker; the leader likes him precisely because the answer is not a speech. One real exchange lands — the leader asks what Eli's mandate is; Eli says the truth (*time-limited, reviewable, and I'd rather be elsewhere*) — and the room goes quiet, because everyone else at the table was appointed for life.
5. Exit: the misfile stands in the record. Later chapters may cite *His Excellency E. Holt, coordinator* without anyone noticing the contradiction — institutional memory preserving its own error.

**Guardrails:** the scene must cost something — the misfile gives the Union delegation a procedural foothold later (they address the compact as an interstate instrument because it was signed under head-of-state protocol), so the comedy is also a legal trap. TBI rules still bind: any word-retrieval failure in the room is frightening, not funny. No slapstick during war chapters; the dinner sits before or after Ch 8's loss, never inside it.

**Standing menu — other misfiled situations (author-selectable; at most one menu beat per volume III–IV, the ambassador dinner is Book III's locked beat and does not count against the cap; never stacked in one chapter):**

- **The queue (Book II–III):** pilgrims or patients treat Somchai's triage as an audience; he runs it like a shift kitchen, calling numbers, refusing to perform miracles on request — gallows register, his own scenes only.
- **The inspection (Book III):** a Union remnant officer arrives to assess the Brazilian network's "command center" and is shown a river dock, a stove, and a shed; the officer files the visit as a confirmed headquarters anyway.
- **The heir audit (Book IV):** court protocol measures **Rowan's** pregnancy against succession calendars — heir mythology is live pressure (messianic-rise lock) — while Rowan, the audited party, and Wren and Asha navigate it from inside the women's court; the comedy is the women's deadpan procedural navigation, not the pregnancy itself. The child inherits no title, and the scene must say so in conduct. (Wren is not the pregnant one in Book IV — her latent-repair pregnancy lands in the IV→V interval; do not misfile the bodies.)
- **The teahouse turn (Book IV, Costa Rica):** the coordinator of the wartime compact waits his turn at Yuliana Mora's teahouse because the house rule is older than his office; he is served last and it is the best thing that happens to him all week.
- **The shift change (Book IV):** Rowan's POV watches Eli dress for court the way he dressed for Foundry shifts — checking pockets, rolling sleeves, nothing ceremonial — while the room treats the gesture as ritual preparation.

**Rule:** the joke is always the category, never the person. If a beat laughs at Eli instead of at the filing system that cannot hold him, cut it.

**The three visitors — Book V cold open, LOCKED 2026-08-06; shelf 2026-08-15.** Wren/Eli's son is born in Book V, after Wren's latent-repair capacity has surfaced unexplained (`60` § *End state*; `99` § *latent repair lock*). Book V is now the founders/infancy volume, so the interval has a shelf: the scene **opens V**, and the Gnostic-misreading material waits for **Book IX** (`62` § *Opening misreading*). It is this generation's own joke — their world's bureaucracies converging on their own birth — and it earns the lighter register a cold open can carry. Do not jump from this farce into Tara-at-twenty in the same binding. Three delegations converge on the birth the same day, each for its own self-interested, unrelated reason, with nobody having coordinated or summoned anyone:

1. **Eastern Mandate** sends a registry officer — official pretext, routine anomaly registration; real reason, the same standing interest security has in any unexplained psi event (`39_psion_biology.md` § *Circuit night as living entrainment ritual*), now aimed at an asset-or-threat evaluation of the child.
2. **Continental Union** sends a protocol liaison — official pretext, diplomatic courtesy given Eli's Book IV civic role; real reason, assessing whether the birth reads as a successor claim worth leverage in the compact (same register as the heir audit above, one bloc over).
3. **Free Ports Coalition** sends no one official — a keeper-house courier already passing through on an unrelated errand stops by out of plain, unbudgeted human curiosity, the only one of the three there for a reason that isn't political. **Never state this asymmetry on-page** — it should just be felt, the same discipline as the civilizational-flaw reading (`59_younger_dryas_nucleation_lock.md` § *The holistic lock*).

Three institutional strangers, arrived independently, standing around the same waiting room, each privately certain their own bloc's presence is the meaningful one — is the whole joke. A tired midwife or nurse treats all three with identical brisk indifference ("you can all wait, all three of you"). Wren and Eli are too exhausted to care and do not perform gratitude, wonder, or discomfort for any of them.

**Guardrails:**

- **No character ever names or notices the Nativity shape.** Not Eli, not Wren, not a narrator aside — the reader gets to have that recognition alone, unassisted, same rule as everywhere else this bible plants a rhyme.
- **The joke is on the three institutions, never on the child or the parents.** No gifts laid at a bedside, no star, no manger staging — keep it a mundane hospital or birthing-house waiting room, not costume.
- **Must still cost something**, per the standing rule above: the Mandate registry filing becomes real paperwork that follows the child later (a line in a registry the family can never fully expunge), so the comedy is also a small, permanent administrative wound, the same shape as the ambassador dinner's legal trap.
- **Does not compete with the heir audit.** That beat is Rowan/Tara's, Book IV, women's-court procedural navigation; this one is Wren's son, the IV→V seam, three-bloc farce. Different mother, different register, different joke — do not merge them.
