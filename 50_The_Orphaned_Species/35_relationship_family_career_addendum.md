## 1. Rowan needs interior access in Book IV, or the pregnancy arc stays half-told

Book IV camera is Wren/Rowan per `60`, but the series POV lock restricts recurring
interior lenses to Eli, Wren, and Somchai. Rowan already passes the away-scene test:
she wants things that are not about helping or opposing Eli, her choices carry costs
that belong partly to her, and reporting them later would destroy dramatic force.

**Minimum on-page requirement:** one scene per major pregnancy beat where Rowan’s
interiority governs and the reader learns what she wants before Eli misreads it.

**Prose test:** the scene must earn its place by changing objective, danger,
knowledge, relationship, or cost — not by supplying the reader with information
Eli lacks.

---

## 2. Wren/Rowan wants that do not route through Eli

The locked relational counterplot is at risk of reading as “two people who share a
partner.” Give each woman a direct want toward the other.

**Wren:** she remembers being left off a manifest. Let that memory show up as a
specific demand on Rowan — not a theme speech, but a request Rowan can refuse.

**Rowan:** her west-corridor Weft and legal/political skill need a private object
inside the Wren relationship — a route she wants Wren to hold, a precedent she
wants Wren to break, a silence she wants Wren to fill.

**On-page test:** in any Wren/Rowan scene, name one thing each wants from the other
before either names what she wants from Eli. If both wants route through Eli, the
bond is costumed, not load-bearing.

---

## 3. Command scenes must stay bodily

Eli’s career identity is facilities work, load, timing, repairs, routes. If Books
III and IV give him offices and titles without a physical action only his background
would produce, the command arc drifts into costume.

**Minimum requirement:** every command scene contains at least one concrete action
rooted in Eli’s idiolect — reading a breaker room instead of a briefing, noticing
heat before the sensor, fixing a latch instead of ordering it fixed, checking a
route with his own hands.

**Dialogue test:** if the scene’s logistics can be handed unchanged to another
character without losing pressure, revise until Eli’s specific perception changes
the outcome.

---

## 4. Pregnancy-as-succession needs mundane intimate texture

Institutional machinery alone will make the Book IV arc feel schematic. Legibility
must show up in domestic refusal.

**Concrete texture checklist — at least two per major succession beat:**
- a corridor worker who will not meet Wren’s eye
- a form with only two adult-name fields
- Eli moving the crib himself because the heir’s room is being prepared without
  asking him
- Wren choosing which chair to sit in when the ranking elders enter
- a meal where nobody says the word “succession” and everyone eats around it

**Revision test:** if a succession scene ends only with a council decision or a
registry action, ask what ordinary refusal was just cut out of frame.

---

## 5. V and IX need one recurring physical gesture for letting-go

“The parents’ test is letting them go” is currently thematic abstraction. The
opening-image and domestic-anchor rules require a concrete body.

**Requirement:** choose one small repeated action that means *I am letting you leave*
in Book V. Possibilities already compatible with existing character vocabulary:
- unlatching a gate and leaving it unlatched
- leaving a tool on a bench instead of carrying it back
- not checking a route he would normally verify

**Payoff rule:** the gesture must reappear in Book IX without exposition. The
reader should recognize it because the body remembers, not because a character
explains what it meant years earlier.

---

## 6. Romance cost must move up or down on the page

`31` § Romance cost audit states the rule; this addendum names the mechanical test.

In any Eli/Wren or Eli/Rowan scene where desire is present, track whether trust,
obligation, or boundary has moved by the scene’s end. If it has not changed, the
scene is wallpaper, regardless of how warm or tense it reads.

**Minimum change per significant romance scene:**
- a hard no that Eli accepts and feels
- a request Eli misreads and later revisits
- a boundary Rowan or Wren draws that Eli cannot undo with charm or competence
- an intimacy Eli refuses because his office can distort consent

The change does not need to be large. It needs to be irreversible within the scene.

---

## 6. Somchai and Shireen: mostly off-page, healing-then-relationship

Shireen remains Bangladesh off-page through Books I–III. She is not a rescue subject, and Eli does not travel to Dhaka in any volume. Her function expands from one voice note to an ongoing encrypted thread—text for logistics, one voice note for memory trade, occasional updates on clinic, remittance, and family pressure—but she never gets sustained on-page scenes with Eli.

Somchai heals Shireen in a brief earned encounter during the Thailand-to-Dhaka corridor stretch in late Book II or Book III. The healing happens before romance: asymmetry is real, debt is paid in ordinary conduct, and the relationship runs on flat ground afterward. Shireen retains her own momentum—clinic work, Dhaka politics, remittance lane—so she is never defined by being Somchai's patient or Eli's cousin.

**Prose test:** any Somchai/Shireen scene must change relationship, danger, knowledge, or cost—not just report that they are together. One shared night during outbreak movement is enough. Keep it lean.

---

## 7. Career identity and family identity must collide in the same scene

Eli’s command, Wren’s network standing, Rowan’s west-corridor role, and the child’s
status all belong to different systems. When they appear in separate scenes, the
reader feels the volume’s politics as separate departments rather than as pressure
on the same bodies.

**Requirement:** at least once per volume, place the career decision and the family
decision in the same room. The command meeting should also be the meal where the
child’s room is being arranged. The registry visit should also be the moment Rowan
and Wren choose whether to trust each other with the truth.

**Test:** if you can describe the volume’s politics without mentioning any specific
body, revise until you cannot.
